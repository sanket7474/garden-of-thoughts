If you have been hiding under a rock and don't know about Last Soul Ultra, congratulations on your peaceful life.

It ended two months ago. I still can't digest it.

It's a race, sort of like a marathon, but with no finish line. <strong>One loop of 6.7 km every hour, on the hour.</strong> You run until you can't.

So guess how many km the last man ran?

<p style={{fontSize: '3.5rem', fontWeight: 800, color: '#ff6b00', lineHeight: 1.1, margin: '0.4em 0'}}>630 km</p>

Yes, you heard it right.

But here is the funny part. The winner is not the one everyone is talking about.

The most hyped man was the runner up. Ayyoub "Ello" El Osrouti. <strong>93 loops.</strong> <span style={{color: '#ff6b00', fontWeight: 700, fontSize: '1.3em'}}>623 km.</span> And then his legs said no.

![Ayyoub "Ello" El Osrouti at Last Soul Ultra 2026](/assets/images/soul-ultra/ello.jpg)

This photo is from loop 94. After almost four days with barely any sleep and a hurting Achilles, his legs simply refused. It was caught by the live stream cameras while more than 120,000 people were watching, which is why it spread everywhere. It was not drama. It was a man giving everything until his body said stop.

---

## The rules (they are simple, which is the problem)

Every hour, on the hour, a new loop of 6.7 km starts. You get exactly one hour to finish it.

Finish in 40 minutes? Great, enjoy 20 minutes of rest. Finish in 59 minutes? Also great, enjoy 1 minute of rest. Be late for the next start? You are out.

There is no finish line and no target distance. The race only ends when one person is left standing.

And here is the cruel part. To win, you must complete <strong>one more loop than everyone else</strong>. So the winner is the only official finisher. Everyone else gets a DNF, even if they ran 93 loops.

Read that again. Ello ran <span style={{color: '#ff6b00', fontWeight: 700, fontSize: '1.3em'}}>623 km</span> and <strong>officially did not finish</strong>.

---

## What actually happened

The race started on a Friday, 14 August 2026, in Germany. Around 160 to 170 runners lined up. Over the next four days they dropped one by one, until only two were left.

Mark Dowdle from the USA. And Ello from Germany. 27 years old.

Dowdle did not come to beat one man.

<Quote author="Mark Dowdle" > I didn't come for 158 souls. I came for all 159.</Quote>

The race is literally called Last Soul Ultra, and the man took the name very seriously.

This was not Ello's first time here. At the first Last Soul Ultra he also ended up second, with 67 loops. So he came back with unfinished business.

And he did not come back to be a background character. By loop 93 he had run roughly <span style={{color: '#ff6b00', fontWeight: 700, fontSize: '1.3em'}}>623 km</span>, which is the second best backyard ultra result by a German, ever. Only Hendrik Boury's 100 loops is ahead of it.

Then loop 94 happened. His legs stopped, and the footage of him struggling and finally collapsing on the grass went around the world. Dowdle finished that last loop in time, and became the winner with <strong>94 loops</strong>, about <span style={{color: '#ff6b00', fontWeight: 700, fontSize: '1.3em'}}>630 km</span>.

---

## Why is everyone talking about Ello?

Honestly, because the winner won because Ello kept going.

Nobody in this race is running against a clock. The only thing that sends you out for the next loop at 3 am is the other person still being out there. Dowdle had to keep going because Ello would not stop. And Ello had to keep going because Dowdle would not stop.

One lost, one won, and both of them ran a distance that most of us will never drive on a bad day.

---

## Your limit is probably just a story

I wrote about this in July, in [Know Your Limits, Then Ignore Them](/blog/thoughts-5/). The idea was simple. Most limits don't live in reality. They live in our thoughts.

Now think about this race.

Ask anyone if a human can run <span style={{color: '#ff6b00', fontWeight: 700, fontSize: '1.3em'}}>623 km</span> in 93 hours with almost no sleep. They will say impossible. Ask them again after August. Two humans did it, in the same forest, at the same time.

Ello is not a superhero. He is a 27 year old who decided that loop 50 is not the end. Then loop 70 is not the end. Then loop 90 is not the end.

Nothing magical happened out there.

Just decision, discipline and courage. One loop at a time.

The winner, Mark Dowdle, said it better than I ever could after the race.

<Quote author="Mark Dowdle"> Every step is evidence that the story in your mind is not true.</Quote>

Read that twice. Every step, not every big dramatic breakthrough. Every boring step in the dark at 3 am counts as proof.

Be honest though. His body did stop at 94. Limits are real, somewhere. But you only find out where they are by walking towards them, and most of us stop at the first fence we imagined ourselves.

So the next time your brain says "I can't", ask one question. <strong>Is this a wall, or just a story I accepted too early?</strong>

Then do one more loop.

---

Don't be afraid of falling. Be afraid of never going far enough to fall.
